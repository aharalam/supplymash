# Structured Problem Research

## Contract
Research **problems, not project concepts**. The team owns ideation. Do not propose apps, features, architectures, or solution concepts unless explicitly requested. Read `BRIEF.md` and official event materials first; collect theme/track, rubric, sponsor, timing, and eligibility constraints. If the event is unknown, request its name or URL after inspecting supplied context.

## Investigation
1. Extract relevant theme/track and judging constraints from official sources, with dates and URLs. Separate confirmed facts from interpretation.
2. Search across independent evidence classes: affected people and firsthand pain reports; technical/academic research; industry or public reports; current solutions and their documented limitations. Prefer primary sources; avoid asserting a market gap from absence of search results. Research broadly before narrowing.
3. Investigate several distinct **problem areas**; describe the underlying failure or unmet need, affected users, frequency/severity where evidenced, why it matters now, and how the track connects. Note any domain access, dataset, API, regulatory, safety, or demo limitations.
4. Rank **research priority**, not winning probability, using evidence strength, theme relevance, accessibility of users/data, and potential to test within the time limit. Never invent universal scoring weights or claim originality.
5. For each problem, append a compact report to `.hackathon/RESEARCH.md`: title; problem statement; affected users; evidence with source URLs and dates; existing approaches and limits; hackathon relevance; testable unknowns; feasibility flags; evidence confidence (`CONFIRMED`, `INFERRED`, `UNKNOWN`); source conflicts. Label illustrative examples as hypothetical.
6. Present findings neutrally. **Stop before ideation** and invite the team to choose problems and propose concepts. When the team supplies an idea, hand off to competition skill for Grill Me and targeted validation.

## Delegation
Optional native subagents may independently research user pain, existing solutions, and technical constraints if tools and time permit. Give nonoverlapping tasks, source requirements, and read-only scope; primary agent synthesizes and resolves conflicting claims. Do not require subagents or debate tournaments.

## Efficiency
Timebox exploration relative to event deadline. Don't block on missing low-priority evidence. If research access is unavailable, state that and leave claims unverified; never manufacture citations.
